# Keep the Rebuild skeleton, tighten its rules

The evidence supports exactly the style the current Rebuild program already uses: three full-body sessions a week built from movement-pattern slots, loaded by double progression (reps first, then weight), with effort held short of failure. Body-part splits, undulating periodization and AI-style per-session generation offer no measurable advantage for this population and carry real costs in session length, missed-day fragility and user trust. So the answer to "should Rebuild be rewritten?" is no: keep the 7-day week, the A/B/C full-body structure, the slot-and-swap design and the four phase names. What needs to change is the rule layer around that skeleton. The first two weeks are too big (up to **21 working sets per session on Day 1**, when the research points to roughly half that at 3–4 reps in reserve), the hinge pattern is trained only once a week, the Tuesday conditioning session is longest in the phase where it should be shortest, and the program has no written rules for load increments, failed sets, returns after a gap, or what counts as a completed day. Adherence research is unambiguous that a 75-day program lives or dies in weeks 2–8, where **about two-thirds of dropouts happen during the ramp-up** ([STRRIDE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9165469/)), so a conservative re-entry, a 20-minute fallback session and a neutral, automatic "lighter day after a gap" are the highest-value additions. The settings question is already solved in principle: one challenge, with the setting changing only the movement in each slot; this report supplies the per-slot defaults for full gym, home gym and bodyweight, a cardio-only track, and limitation swaps for back, knee and shoulder framed as comfort preferences rather than medical rules.

## Full-body pattern slots beat splits for someone training three days a week

The strongest finding in the strength literature for this decision is that, once weekly sets are equal, how you divide them across the week barely matters for muscle growth. The 2019 Schoenfeld meta-analysis of 25 studies concluded that training frequency "does not significantly or meaningfully impact muscle hypertrophy when volume is equated" ([Schoenfeld 2019 summary](https://mennohenselmans.com/?p=8631)), and two randomized trials in untrained adults found no difference between full-body and split layouts when weekly sets matched ([Pedersen 2022](https://bmcsportsscimedrehabil.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13102-022-00481-7); [Evangelista 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8372753/)). For strength there is a small tilt toward hitting a lift more often: effect sizes rose from **0.74 to 1.08 across 1 to 4+ sessions per week**, driven by multi-joint upper-body lifts, but the effect disappeared in volume-equated studies ([Grgic 2018](https://sportsmedicine-open.springeropen.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s40798-018-0149-9)). Physiology, in other words, is a tie. The decision is made by practical factors, and all of them favour full-body sessions for a three-day returner. Each movement pattern gets two or three practice exposures a week, which matters because novices need "proficiency in resistance training movement patterns" before loading climbs ([NSCA PTQ](https://dxpprod.nsca.com/contentassets/3d09f06f0b4c4f6fbd8cc382ed1f3d4a/ptq-10.2.1-progressive-strategies-for-teaching-fundamental-resistance-training-movement-patterns.pdf)). A missed Wednesday costs one-third of one week's volume for every muscle, rather than the entire week's volume for one body region, which is the only layout compatible with "missed a day? continue." And an upper/lower split needs four days to train each half twice, so at three days it drifts into uneven weeks.

Pattern-based slots are the right unit of generation for the same reason. Every coaching curriculum the research found converges on the same taxonomy (squat, hinge, lunge or single-leg, horizontal and vertical push, horizontal and vertical pull, carry, core), and World Rugby's coaching passport states outright that these patterns "provide the coach with a readymade resistance training template" ([World Rugby](https://passport.world.rugby/conditioning-for-rugby/advanced-conditioning-for-rugby-pre-level-2/resistance-training/the-gym-based-training-programme/movement-patterns/)). The substitution principle is equally consistent: swap a push for a push, a hinge for a hinge, and preferably a horizontal pull for another horizontal pull ([CrossFit substitution guide](https://morningchalkup.com/2020/03/04/crossfit-movement-substitution-guide/)). This is practitioner consensus rather than trial evidence, and the notes are explicit that no study has tested a pattern template loaded with different equipment. But it rests on two findings that do have trial support: hypertrophy is independent of load across 28 studies, so a dumbbell or kettlebell taken close enough to effort produces what a barbell produces ([Lopez 2021](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/75929)), and the minimum effective program is simply a leg press or squat, an upper push and an upper pull, with single-joint work "unlikely" to add meaningful benefit ([Iversen 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8449772)). That last point has a direct design consequence: the quad, triceps, biceps and lateral-raise slots in the current program are legitimate for preference and enjoyment, but they should be labelled as optional finishers that come after the pattern slots and are dropped first when time is short.

The app-market research confirms the choice from the other direction. Per-session generators like Fitbod win on interface and equipment filtering but draw recurring complaints that workouts feel "randomized rather than strategically tailored" and that progression is weak ([Dr. Muscle review, a competitor](https://dr-muscle.com/fitbod-app-review-alternative/)); template-plus-rules apps like Boostcamp and StrongLifts earn praise for predictable progression ([Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/boostcamp-review)). A fixed slot template with deterministic rules and equipment-driven fill, which is what Rebuild already is, sits in the gap between those two complaint lists. Boostcamp's prompt on every swap, "apply to today only or to all future workouts", is the same semantics Onward already ships, which is reassuring validation of an existing decision ([Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/boostcamp-review)).

## Start at half the dose, stop two reps short, add weight only when reps allow

Volume has a floor and a steep early curve. Iversen's review recommends **a minimum of 4 weekly sets per muscle group**, with around 10 if time permits, and notes that in untrained people a single set three times a week is enough to produce gains ([Iversen 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8449772)). Schoenfeld's dose-response analysis found each additional weekly set adds roughly **0.37% muscle growth**, with 10+ sets per week outperforming 5–9 ([Schoenfeld 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/)), and a 67-study preprint found diminishing returns arrive much sooner for strength than for size ([Pelland preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460)). Renaissance Periodization's practitioner landmark puts maintenance at about 6 sets per muscle per week and advises adding roughly one set per week while recovery is good ([RP volume landmarks](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/training-volume-landmarks-muscle-growth)). Put together, the defensible shape for 75 days is 4–6 hard sets per pattern in weeks 1–2, climbing to 8–12 by weeks 6–10. The current Rebuild phase overshoots that start: seven slots at three sets each is 21 sets on Day 1, and because the squat, press and pull patterns appear in two or three sessions, weekly pattern volume in week one already sits at the level the evidence reserves for week six.

The reason to start low is soreness and dropout, not stimulus. Conventional strength training is among the safest activities measured, at **0.24–1 injury per 1,000 hours** ([Keogh & Winwood 2017 via NSCA](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/ptq/the-safest-and-riskiest-forms-of-resistance-training/)), but the repeated bout effect means the first exposure to each movement is where soreness concentrates, and a single low-volume bout protects against it for weeks ([Hyldahl, Chen & Nosaka 2017](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=AUTH:%22Hyldahl%20RD%22%20AND%20TITLE:%22repeated%20bout%22&resultType=core&format=json)). Barbell Medicine's return-to-gym protocol operationalizes this as "never exceed RPE 8 during the first three weeks", starting at three sets of four to ten reps and adding one set a week ([Barbell Medicine](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/returning-to-the-gym/)). Week one in Onward should therefore be two sets per pattern slot at three to four reps in reserve, which banks the protective effect of every movement before volume rises. Former athletes are the group most likely to override this, which is precisely why the app should hold the line for them.

Effort targets are the easiest part to get right because the recent evidence is one-directional. Training to failure is not required: a 2026 meta-analysis of 20 studies found non-failure training slightly better for dynamic strength (SMD 0.24) and equal for hypertrophy ([Wu 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22repetition%20non-failure%22&resultType=core&format=json)), and a within-subject trial showed 1–3 reps in reserve produced the same growth and 1RM gains as failure ([Vasconcelos 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22repetitions-in-reserve%22%20AND%20AUTH:%22Refalo%20MC%22&resultType=core&format=json)). Strength gains were similar across a wide range of RIR in the Robinson meta-regression, while hypertrophy improved modestly as sets got closer to failure ([Robinson 2024](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/publications/exploring-the-dose-response-relationship-between-estimated-resist/)). The catch is that people estimate RIR badly exactly where a comeback program would use it: accuracy falls as reps rise and load drops ([Russo 2025](https://dspace.uevora.pt/rdpc/bitstream/10174/39438/1/2025%20Russo%20et%20al.%20Factors%20influencing%20the%20accuracy%20of%20the%20repetition%20in%20reserve%20scale%20in%20resistance%20training%20%20a%20systematic%20review.pdf)). So the program should cap rep ranges at about 12 when it asks for an RIR judgment, phrase the target as "stop with 2 clean reps left" rather than a numeric scale, and ask for 1 RIR only on the final set of the last phase. Rebuild's existing phase copy ("RPE 6–7, leave 3–4 reps in reserve" rising to "around RPE 8") is already correct; it just needs to be the rule the app enforces, not a sentence the user reads.

Progression should stay double progression, and the research gives it unusually direct support: a 10-week trial in untrained adults found progressing by load and progressing by reps produced the same strength and size gains ([Chaves 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38286426/)), and the ACSM's landmark rule is to add **2–10% load only when the target reps can be exceeded by one or two** ([ACSM 2009](https://www.sportgeneeskunde.com/wp-content/uploads/ACSM-Position-Stand-Progression-Models-in-Resistance-Training-for-Healthy-Adults.pdf)). This scheme tolerates the founder's 5-lb dumbbell steps (a 5–10% jump at the loads a returner uses), and a bad day simply yields fewer reps instead of a failed weight. RPE-based autoregulation matches percentage-based loading in trained lifters but has never been tested in novices ([Hickmott 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8762534/)), so it belongs as a governor ("if the warm-up feels heavy, repeat last session's weight"), not as the load-selection rule. The six open beginner programs surveyed all converge on the same failure rule, a **10% reduction after two or three consecutive misses**, and StrongLifts' app additionally deloads automatically "if you're stuck or just had a break" ([StrongLifts](https://stronglifts.com/app/); [thefitness.wiki](https://thefitness.wiki/routines/r-fitness-basic-beginner-routine/)). That is the rule Onward should adopt verbatim.

Phase structure can stay as four named blocks, but the evidence says the blocks should differ by volume and effort, not by style. Periodized programs beat non-periodized ones for 1RM by a small margin (ES 0.31 in volume-equated studies), with no hypertrophy difference ([2022 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/)), and undulating versus linear periodization is a wash in beginners (ES 0.06) ([sci-sport summary](https://sci-sport.com/en/effects-intensity-volume-periodization-strength-hypertrophy-238/)). A planned week of complete rest mid-program did not help growth and slightly hurt strength in the only RCT ([Coleman 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10809978/)), so Rebuild should not schedule a deload week; it should trigger lighter sessions from missed days, falling reps or the daily check-in. Returners also recover faster than novices: older men needed **fewer than eight weeks of retraining** to regain strength lost over a 12-week layoff ([Blocquiaux 2020](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=AUTH:%22Blocquiaux%20S%22%20AND%20TITLE:detraining&resultType=core&format=json)), and about 60% of a strength gain survived 20 weeks of detraining ([Psilander 2019](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A1336656/FULLTEXT01.pdf)). The practical reading is that a former athlete should expect to approach old working weights by Build or Push, and the ramp should be conservative on volume and effort rather than on exercise complexity.

## Weeks two to eight decide everything, so make them hard to fail

The adherence literature reframes the whole program design. In the STRRIDE trials, 31% of 947 adults dropped out, and **about 67% of those dropouts left before or during the ramp-up** to the prescribed dose; once participants survived the ramp, adherence "did not waiver" for the following six to eight months, and lack of time was the top reason (40%) for leaving ([STRRIDE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9165469/)). A modified Couch-to-5K study saw only 27% finish, with most quitting between weeks two and five, 19% reporting an injury, and interviewees saying the plan "progress[es] too aggressively for true beginners" ([IJERPH 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/20/17/6682)). A 75-day calendar spans this entire danger zone. The design response is to make weeks one through three short, easy and nearly impossible to fail, to ship a 20–25-minute fallback version of every session so a crowded day produces a reduced workout rather than a miss, and to deliver the real training dose in Build and Push, after the routine has survived.

Onward's missed-day philosophy is directly supported. Lally's habit-formation study found that "missing a single opportunity to perform the behavior did not materially affect the overall habit formation process", while many misses spread over time did lower the eventual ceiling ([BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit)). Broken streaks "measurably suppress subsequent engagement, and the suppression is durable", and participants allowed to keep a streak alive through two different activity types were significantly more motivated than those restricted to one ([UDel / Silverman](https://udel.edu/udaily/2024/march/power-of-streaks-motivation-jackie-silverman)). Duolingo's largest retention gain came from decoupling the streak from the full daily goal: one lesson keeps the streak, goal progress shows separately, worth **+3.3% 14-day retention** ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). The relapse-prevention literature names the mechanism Onward is avoiding, the abstinence violation effect, in which guilt after one lapse turns into quitting ([ACSM-CEP guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/acsm-cep/education-behavior/adherence-relapse)); 75 Hard's "back to day one" rule is the textbook trigger, and experts quoted in 2026 coverage warn its "rigid, all-or-nothing approach may undermine the long-term behavioral changes it promises" ([CNN via ABC17](https://abc17news.com/cnn-health/2026/03/21/should-you-try-the-75-hard-challenge-experts-warn-the-risks-may-outweigh-the-benefits/)). Three concrete rules follow. A day counts as complete if the user does the planned session, the short version, or a rest day with three or more habits ticked; full-session progress displays separately. Counters should be "days completed out of days so far" and "weeks with two or more sessions", never consecutive days that reset. And after three or more consecutive missed training days, the next session should arrive automatically as a lighter re-entry with neutral copy ("Day 13 is Strength B, 25 minutes today"), because only human-coached apps such as Future currently do this well, with a reviewer noting the coach "adjusted the third workout to allow for it instead of shaming them" ([Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/nutrition/future-fitness-review)).

Flexibility helps when it is bounded. Autonomous motivation consistently predicts exercise adherence across 66 studies ([Teixeira 2012](https://ijbnpa.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1479-5868-9-78)), and the one strength trial of a readiness-based flexible template (rate your energy, pick the matching session) beat a fixed order on leg-press strength ([Stronger by Science summary](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=45619)). But a pragmatic RCT that let people choose between HIIT and moderate cardio found no benefit for motivation or adherence ([University of Alberta trial](https://era.library.ualberta.ca/items/82127786-244c-402a-93fd-94a8952c95a7/download/96f995ee-782e-4da0-b379-2fd2cda92d26)). The menu, then, is small: the planned session, the short version, an easier version, and a movement swap, always with a default pre-filled. The five daily habits are a self-monitoring tool, which Michie's meta-regression of 122 evaluations identified as the most effective behaviour-change technique, with effect size rising from 0.26 to **0.42 when paired with goal setting, feedback and goal review** ([Michie 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19916637/)). Onward already has the checklist, the weight chart and the week summary; the missing piece is light goal review, a weekly prompt that compares this week to last and lets the user adjust. Weight-loss expectations need the same honesty: self-weighing improves outcomes without psychological harm whether daily or weekly ([PMC4546162](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4546162/)), digital programs set 0.5–2 lb per week ([JMIR 2019](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2019/2/e12209/PDF)), and cardio alone produced roughly 1–3 kg of fat loss in 10–12-week trials ([Keating 2017](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:28513103&resultType=core&format=json)). The goal line on the chart should slope at 0.5–1 lb per week, meaning 6–11 lb over the program, rather than drawing the full 15–20 lb target at Day 75 and scheduling a failure.

## Conditioning: moderate by default, intervals as a layer, steps as the floor

Fat loss does not depend on the cardio format. Across 31 studies, HIIT and moderate continuous training produced the same body-fat change (−1.26% versus −1.48%) and neither delivered clinically meaningful short-term loss ([Keating 2017](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:28513103&resultType=core&format=json)). What does separate the formats is how they feel: intensity above the ventilatory threshold worsens in-session affect in inactive and overweight adults, and shorter or easier intervals improve it ([Tempest et al.](https://nrl.northumbria.ac.uk/46483/1/Tempest_PMS-21-0010.R2.jdb.%5B1%5D.pdf)), while unsupervised adherence runs about 63% for HIIT and 68% for moderate work ([Santos et al. 2023](https://ijbnpa.biomedcentral.com:443/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12966-023-01535-w.pdf)). Intervals still earn a place because they raise VO2max more (4×4-minute efforts raised it 7.2% in eight weeks against no change for long slow distance) ([Helgerud 2007](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:17414804&resultType=core&format=json)) and because combined strength plus aerobic training ranked first for cardiometabolic health across 81 trials ([Batrakoulis 2022](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/comparative-efficacy-of-5-exercise-types-on-cardiometabolic-healt/)). Interference is not a concern at this dose: the 43-trial update found hypertrophy and maximal strength untouched by concurrent cardio, with only explosive power affected and only when cardio sat within three hours of lifting ([Schumann 2022](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Compatibility-of-Concurrent-Aerobic-and-Strength-Training.pdf)). Rebuild's 3 strength + 2 conditioning + 1 easy + 1 rest week therefore matches the evidence, and the easy Thursday is its single most valuable adherence feature, because it has the highest enjoyment, lowest injury demand, and absorbs a missed Tuesday or Saturday without any restart.

The ramp needs to follow "duration before intensity". ACSM-based guidance for returning exercisers starts at 15–20 minutes at RPE 11–13, adds 5–10 minutes every one to two weeks, and holds increases near 10% per week ([ACSM-CPT guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/acsm-cpt/chapter-5-cardiorespiratory-programming/cardiorespiratory-progression-periodization)); Couch to 5K's nine-week ramp from 60-second runs to 30 continuous minutes, with explicit permission to repeat a week, is the best-known applied version ([Couch to 5K](https://healthwell.eani.org.uk/node/2247); [repeat rule](https://www.findyouredge.app/news/couch-to-5k-beginner-guide)). Rebuild currently inverts this on Tuesday: the Rebuild-phase Peloton session is the longest of the program at about 40 minutes, then doubles interval count from 5 to 10 rounds at Day 15. The fix is a 20–25-minute Tuesday in weeks one and two, growing to 30–35 by Perform, with intervals climbing 5 → 8 → 10 → 12 rounds. The founder's heavy bag is a legitimate interval modality at roughly **8 kcal per minute and 156 bpm** in ten 3-minute rounds ([CSU Ohio record](https://facultyprofile.csuohio.edu/en/publications/a-comparison-of-energy-expenditure-during-wii-boxing-versus-heavy-18/)), but its injury literature is about sparring, not bag work, so the only bag-specific inference is that rotational trunk load makes the bike the sensible default swap when the low-back toggle is on. The "repeat a week" affordance translates cleanly to Onward: the calendar always advances, but after a gap the prescribed stimulus holds at the previous week's level.

Steps are the floor beneath all of this. The 2025 Lancet Public Health dose-response analysis found benefits rise steeply to about 5,000–7,000 steps a day and flatten afterward, with 7,000 steps associated with **47% lower all-cause mortality** than 2,000 and 10,000 still "a viable target for those who are more active" ([Ding 2025](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:40713949&resultType=core&format=json)). Onward's 8,000 sits comfortably between the inflection point and convention; the onboarding generator can start new users at 6,000–7,000 and nudge upward, and there is no evidence-based reason to lower the target on lifting days. For the daily habits more broadly, the simpler behaviours (steps, sleep, a protein or meal-plan tick) reach automaticity faster than exercise itself (medians of 59–66 days against an exercise estimate of about 91) ([Behavioral Scientist on Lally](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/how-long-to-form-a-habit); [2024 meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)), which is why they belong in the checklist as early wins. Keep the list at five; every additional tracker feeds the "lack of time" dropout reason, and diet logging belongs in a dedicated app mirrored by a single tick.

## One challenge, many settings: swap the movement, never the rule

The decision already taken, that the setting changes only the movement in each slot, is the industry norm and the evidence-consistent one. Every app surveyed filters a library by owned equipment, and the ones that offer presets use roughly the same three (Hevy's "At home / Dumbbells only / Gym / Bodyweight" categories; Caliber's gym, home gym and bodyweight-only program) ([Hevy](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/36011518408983); [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/caliber-app-review)). The differentiator reviewers actually ask for is load awareness: Fitbod lets users specify dumbbell weights but not barbell or kettlebell loads, which a reviewer called "problematic in home gyms" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/fitbod-app-review)). Onward should record the user's owned dumbbell and kettlebell loads so that "add the smallest increment" means the next pair on the rack, and reps carry the progression when the next bell is a 10-lb jump. For the bodyweight profile the r/bodyweightfitness Recommended Routine supplies a machine-ready rule: start each movement at 3×5, add a rep per set per session to 3×8, then advance to the next variation on an ordered ladder and restart at 3×5 ([Recommended Routine](https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/recommended-routine)). Rep progression has peer-reviewed support as a true overload path ([Plotkin 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36199287/)). Every slot's substitution table should therefore carry a bodyweight ladder, not a single bodyweight exercise, so progression never dead-ends.

Limitations should be handled as training preferences, and the clinical evidence makes that framing honest rather than evasive. For low back pain, Cochrane's 249-trial review finds exercise effective with adverse effects that are mostly minor soreness ([PEDro record of Hayden 2021](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/66912)), and resistance training had the highest probability of being best for physical function across 89 studies ([Owen 2020](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/54/21/1279)). The "is spinal flexion dangerous" debate is a practitioner disagreement with, in the words of the pro-neutral-spine summary, no studies "comparing lifters squatting or deadlifting appreciable weights with a neutral vs. flexed spine" on injury rates ([Henselmans](https://mennohenselmans.com/is-spinal-flexion-dangerous/)). After fusion, surgeon-authored guidance permits a full return to bending, lifting and twisting once the fusion is solid, typically around six months ([Spine-health](https://www.spine-health.com/treatment/spinal-fusion/spinal-fusion-surgery-recovery-3-months-and-after)), and a physician-coach reports training post-fusion clients through deadlifts while advising coaches to "avoid pairing your coaching with unnecessary messages of danger" ([Barbell Medicine forum](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/exercising-post-fully-healed-lumbar-fusion/7681)). For knees, guideline-level support for hip and quad strengthening is strong ([JOSPT 2019 CPG summary](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/1001/p442.pdf)) and exercise matched arthroscopy at ten years for degenerative meniscus ([OMEX 10-year](https://www.physiotutors.com/research/meniscectomy-or-exercise-for-degenerative-meniscus/)), while squat-depth biomechanics are mixed, which supports a "lighten first, then shorten range, then swap" order. For shoulders, graded loading equals surgery for several diagnoses but about 30% of people do not respond ([Lewis protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7646354)), and the swap set (rows, neutral grip, half-kneeling landmine press that "often lets athletes train above 90 degrees of shoulder elevation without pain") is sports-physio consensus ([Barbell Physio](https://thebarbellphysio.com/training-shoulder-pain/)). The product pattern to copy is Fitbod's: a "work around" feature that adjusts exercise selection, paired with a "not medical advice; consult a professional" line ([Fitbod help](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/37629269518103)), plus a Peloton-style self-affirmation that the user's physician has cleared them ([Peloton terms](https://conductatlas.com/platform/peloton/peloton-terms-of-service/user-health-fitness-affirmation-requirement/)). Apple's guideline 1.4.1 applies "greater scrutiny" to apps that could be used for diagnosing or treating and expects a reminder to check with a doctor ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), so Hinge-Health-style contraindication lists signal a medical device and should be avoided.

## Decision and program specification

**Decision.** Do not rewrite Rebuild. Keep the 7-day calendar (Strength A / Conditioning / Strength B / Recovery / Strength C / Conditioning / Rest), the three full-body pattern-slot sessions with swap lists, the four phase names, the double-progression rule and the five habits. Revise the rule layer as specified below, and treat the founder's garage program as the "home gym" instance of the general template rather than as the template itself. Everything in this section is a synthesis of the cited evidence; the notes are explicit that no trial has tested this exact 75-day structure, so the numbers are defensible defaults to be checked against founder and tester data, not laws.

### Weekly structure and session templates

| Day | Session | Length target | Core pattern slots (in order) | Optional finisher slots |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Strength A | 35–45 min (short version 20–25) | Squat (bilateral, knee-dominant) · Horizontal push · Horizontal pull · Single-leg · Core, anti-extension | Quad isolation · Triceps |
| Tue | Conditioning 1 | 20–35 min by phase | Easy base plus short intervals on bike (or walk/jog, rower) | — |
| Wed | Strength B | 35–45 min (short 20–25) | Single-leg / lunge · Vertical push · Vertical pull · Hinge (main) · Carry | Biceps |
| Thu | Recovery | 30–60 min | Easy Zone 2 bike or walk, RPE 3–5; counts toward steps | 10 min mobility (habit) |
| Fri | Strength C | 35–45 min (short 20–25) | Squat (variant) · Horizontal push (variant) · Horizontal pull (variant) · Hinge (second exposure, glute-dominant or lighter) · Core, anti-rotation or side | Shoulders (lateral raise) |
| Sat | Conditioning 2 | 25–35 min by phase | Rounds: bag, bike, or optional pattern circuit | — |
| Sun | Full rest | — | Normal daily movement | — |

The short version of each strength session is the first four core slots at the phase's set count minus one (minimum two sets), no finishers, and it counts as a completed day. The main change to pattern coverage is that the hinge appears twice a week (Strength B main hinge, Strength C lighter hinge), replacing C's second row; pulls still get three exposures via A, B and C.

### Per-phase prescription

| Phase | Days | Sets per core slot | Finisher sets | Rep range (main / accessory) | Effort (reps in reserve) | Rest | What changes at the boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rebuild | 1–7 | 2 | 0–1 | 8–12 / 10–15 | 3–4 ("stop with 3–4 easy reps left") | 90 s | Movement exposure only; loads deliberately under capacity (last known lift minus about 20%, or bodyweight / first bell) |
| Rebuild | 8–14 | 3 on slots 1–3, 2 on slots 4–5 | 1 | 8–12 / 10–15 | 3 | 90 s | Sets rise; loads and effort hold |
| Build | 15–35 | 3 | 2 | 8–12 / 10–15 | 2–3 | 90–120 s | Effort tightens by one rep; sets rise by one on slots 4–5 only |
| Push | 36–55 | 4 on slots 1–3, 3 on slots 4–5 | 2–3 | 6–10 / 10–15 | 2 | 2–3 min on slot 1, else 90–120 s | Sets rise and rep range drops; effort holds |
| Perform | 56–75 | 4 on slots 1–3, 3 on slots 4–5 | 2–3 | 6–10 / 10–15 | 2, with 1 on the last set of slots 1–3 only; never to failure | Same | Effort tightens; sets hold; final week (days 70–75) repeats loads with no new increments and ends with a "best since Day 1" summary |

Weekly hard sets per pattern under this table land at roughly 4–6 in Rebuild, 9 in Build, and 11–14 in Push and Perform, which tracks the Iversen floor and the Schoenfeld dose curve. Never raise sets and effort for the same slot in the same week; the table alternates them deliberately.

### Progression, failure and lighter-day rules

Load progression is double progression on every weighted slot: when all working sets reach the top of the rep range at the target effort, add the smallest owned increment next session, capped at 10% (barbell +5 lb upper / +10 lb lower; dumbbells the next owned pair; kettlebells the next bell, and if that jump exceeds 10%, keep adding reps up to 15 before stepping). Bodyweight slots use the 3×5 → 3×8 → next-variation ladder. If working reps fall below the bottom of the range on the same lift two sessions in a row, reduce that lift's load 10% and rebuild; only that lift resets. Readiness governor: if the daily check-in reports poor sleep or high soreness, or the first working set feels heavier than last time, the app offers "repeat last session's weight" as the default for that day. Lighter-day triggers, all automatic and all phrased neutrally: after 3–6 consecutive missed training days, the next strength session is a re-entry (2 sets per core slot, loads −10%, 3 RIR, no finishers) and conditioning repeats the previous week's prescription; after 7 or more days away, the next full week runs at the previous phase's prescription before the calendar's phase resumes; two consecutive sessions with falling reps on two or more lifts trigger one session at half the sets with unchanged loads. There is no scheduled deload week.

Cardio modalities map the same way: the bike is the default where one exists, walk/jog or rower otherwise, and the heavy bag is an interval option offered only when the user has one and the low-back toggle is off.

### The cardio track

Tuesday (Conditioning 1) runs 5 min easy warm-up, an easy block, intervals, and 5 min cool-down: Rebuild 20–25 min total with 4–5 × 30 s brisk / 90 s easy at RPE 6–7; Build 25–30 min with 6–8 × 45 s / 75 s; Push 30 min with 8–10 × 60 s / 60 s; Perform 30–35 min with 10–12 × 60 s / 60 s or 3–4 × 3 min "hard but controlled" with 3 min easy between. Saturday (Conditioning 2) is round-based: bag 4–6 × 2 min / 1 min at RPE 6 in Rebuild, 6–8 rounds in Build, 8–10 in Push, 10 in Perform; the bike equivalents are 6 × 30/90, 8 × 45/75, 10 × 60/60, 12 × 60/60; the pattern circuit stays optional and is listed last. Thursday stays an easy 30–60 minutes at conversational pace and counts toward the step habit. Total structured time reaches roughly 150–200 minutes a week by Push, which is where the guideline range begins.

The cardio-only track (for users with no strength equipment, or who choose it) keeps the same calendar and habits and swaps sessions: Mon and Fri become 20-minute bodyweight pattern sessions (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry slots at the bodyweight ladders, 2–3 sets), Tue and Sat are the interval sessions above on bike, walk/jog or rower, Thu is the easy day, Wed is a 20–30-minute easy session that grows by 5 minutes per phase, and Sun is rest. Weeks 1–3 build duration only (15 → 25 min easy, RPE 11–13 on the 6–20 scale), weeks 4–6 add one Couch-to-5K-style interval day, weeks 7–9 add the second interval day and extend the easy day to 30–40 min, and weeks 10–11 introduce 3–4 × 3 min efforts once a week. Bookend it with a self-test in week 1 and week 11 (timed 2 km walk or run, or a 20-minute bike distance) so the track has its own "best since Day 1".

### Settings and limitation handling

Three equipment profiles (full gym; home gym with dumbbells/kettlebells, bench and optional bar; bodyweight/travel) plus the cardio-only track cover the market presets. Onboarding records owned dumbbell and kettlebell loads. Limitations are "work around" toggles for lower back, knee and shoulder, each of which changes slot defaults and adds cues but leaves every original movement available in the swap list. The order of adjustment is always lighten the load, then shorten the range, then swap the movement. Lower back: squat defaults to goblet or box squat, main hinge to glute bridge or hip thrust with the RDL kept as a swap ("only if it feels completely fine"), rows to chest-supported, carries kept, conditioning default to bike rather than bag, no loaded flexion or jumps in circuits. Knee: squat to box squat at a chosen height, single-leg to step-up with an adjustable box or reverse lunge, cycling as the conditioning default, quad isolation kept only if comfortable. Shoulder: horizontal push to floor or incline neutral-grip DB press before flat barbell, vertical push to half-kneeling landmine press, pulling weighted toward rows, carries to bottoms-up or front-rack. The post-fusion case (the founder's) is the lower-back toggle plus a one-time self-declared "my doctor has cleared me for unrestricted exercise" affirmation, with no hard-coded restriction.

### Copy and framing rules

Phrase effort as reps left in the tank, not a numeric scale, and cap at "2 clean reps left" except the final set in Perform. Describe swaps as comfort and preference ("many people with a back history find front-loaded squats and supported rows feel better"), never as protection or safety; avoid "protects", "safe for your spine", "rehab", "treats", "corrects". Keep the stop rule to two sentences: "Stop and swap any movement that causes sharp or spreading pain, numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm or leg. For numbness around the groin or changes in bladder or bowel control, see a doctor urgently", which mirrors NHS patient leaflets rather than diagnosis ([NHSGGC leaflet](https://rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/media/301h1exb/cauda-equina-syndrome-important-warning-signs-nhsggc.pdf)). Missed-day copy is neutral and forward-looking ("Day 12 skipped. Day 13 is Strength B, about 25 minutes") and the lighter re-entry session is presented as the plan, not as a consequence. Frame personal records as "best since Day 1", never lifetime bests. Set weight expectations at 0.5–1 lb per week with a rolling-average trend line. Present the 75 days as "the foundation of a habit", not "after 75 days it is automatic", since exercise habits have medians near 91 days. Include one visible "check with your doctor before starting if you have been inactive or were told to limit activity" line in onboarding and settings, and nowhere else.

## Conclusion

The most useful thing the research changes is where the design effort goes. The program's training logic was already sound; the evidence simply confirms that full-body pattern slots, double progression and sub-failure effort are the right defaults for a deconditioned adult, and that the alternatives (splits, undulation, per-session AI generation, scheduled deloads) buy nothing measurable here. What the evidence adds is a sharper sense of where a 75-day comeback actually fails: in the first three weeks, through soreness and time pressure, and in the emotional moment after a missed day. That means the re-entry dose, the short-session fallback, the automatic lighter day and the "today counts" rule are not polish on top of the program; they are the product's core mechanism, and the "missed a day? Onward" tagline is a claim the app should be able to make because its rules enforce it.

The remaining uncertainty is honest and worth tracking. No trial has tested a two-week-then-build structure, a pattern template across equipment profiles, or any missed-day rule in returning adults; the numbers above are synthesized defaults. Onward's own data (founder first, then ten testers) is the right place to check them, and the metrics that matter are not strength curves but whether people are still training in week six, how often the short version rescues a day, and how quickly users come back after a gap. If those three numbers look good, the program is working regardless of what the lifts say.
